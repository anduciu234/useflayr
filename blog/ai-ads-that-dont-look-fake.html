How to make AI ads that don't look fake

The biggest fear with AI ads is that they'll look obviously fake. Here's how to generate AI ad creative that viewers can't distinguish from human-made content.

7 min read Creative Quality
Quick answer

AI ads stop looking fake when you lead with photo ads over video, keep any video under 15 seconds, focus on the hook instead of the avatar's realism, curate ruthlessly instead of mass-publishing, and match the creative style to the platform it's running on.

The number one objection to AI-generated ad creative is always the same: "but it looks fake." And honestly, a lot of it does. The stiff avatar movements, the uncanny valley faces, the lighting that feels slightly off. Bad AI creative is immediately recognizable and using it as a paid ad is essentially burning money.

But here's the thing: the gap between good AI creative and bad AI creative is enormous, and most of it comes down to choices the user makes, not limitations of the technology.

Here's how to consistently produce AI ads that don't trigger the "this is AI" alarm.

Start with photo ads, not video

This is the single most important tactical decision. AI-generated photo ads have reached a point where they're genuinely indistinguishable from professional photography for most viewers. The technology for static images is 6-12 months ahead of video generation.

If you're worried about AI quality, lead with photo ads. A well-generated product photo with a model, natural lighting, and clean composition will outperform a mediocre AI video every time. Once you're comfortable with photo ad performance, expand to video.

We've run this experiment at Flayr dozens of times: show someone a generated photo ad and ask if it's real. The success rate is over 90% for photo ads. For video, it's closer to 70%. That gap matters when you're putting money behind the creative.

Choose the right video length

Every additional second of video increases the chance of something looking off. AI-generated video at 8-12 seconds is much more convincing than AI video at 30-45 seconds.

For paid ads, shorter is almost always better anyway. TikTok's own data shows that the most effective ads deliver the key message within the first 6 seconds. You don't need a 60-second testimonial. You need a 10-second hook that stops the scroll and delivers one compelling reason to click.

Keep AI video ads between 8-15 seconds. At this length, the quality of current models like Seedance 2.0 is strong enough that most viewers won't notice it's AI. The occasional imperfection gets lost in the fast-paced viewing context.

The hook matters more than the face

Here's a counterintuitive truth: viewers don't scrutinize the person in an ad nearly as much as creators think they do. They scrutinize the message. If your hook is compelling enough, the viewer's attention goes to the words, the product, and the offer, not to whether the person's micro-expressions are perfectly natural.

A mediocre-looking AI avatar with a killer hook outperforms a photorealistic AI avatar with a weak hook every single time. Focus your energy on the script and the first 2 seconds, not on finding the most realistic avatar.

This is partly why competitor intelligence matters so much. When Flayr generates an ad, the hook formula comes from analyzing what's already working for competitors. The generation quality is important, but the hook selection is what determines whether someone watches or scrolls.

Use the right backgrounds and settings

One of the most common AI tells is the background. Generic, overly perfect backgrounds scream "AI-generated" even when the person looks natural. A flawless white studio background with perfect lighting and zero imperfections doesn't look real because real environments have texture, shadows, and slight imperfections.

The best AI ads use realistic, slightly imperfect environments: a living room with natural light from a window, a kitchen counter with visible items in the background, an outdoor setting with natural depth of field. These contexts add believability that a clean studio background can't provide.

Don't over-generate

Another common mistake: generating 50 variations hoping to find one good one. This leads to publishing mediocre content because you've spent so long generating that you start to lose judgment about what's good and what's not.

Generate 5-8 variations. Review each one critically. Would you scroll past this? Does anything look off in the first 2 seconds? Would you know this was AI if you didn't know? If you hesitate on any of these, regenerate or move on to a different format.

Quality filtering is the most important step in the entire process. The AI will produce some output that looks great and some that looks obviously fake. Your job is curation, not mass production.

Match the platform context

An ad that looks natural on TikTok might look fake on a Meta feed placement, and vice versa. TikTok's aesthetic is raw, slightly chaotic, low-production-value by design. AI creative that's too polished actually performs worse on TikTok because it doesn't match the organic content surrounding it.

For TikTok: slightly shaky framing, casual speech patterns, reaction-style formats. The imperfections of AI generation can actually help here because they add the rawness that TikTok audiences expect.

For Meta feed: cleaner compositions, more polished product shots, professional-looking lifestyle imagery. Meta's feed environment is more curated, so the creative needs to match that context.

For Meta Stories/Reels: somewhere in between. Full-screen vertical, slightly more casual than feed, but more structured than TikTok.

The technical details that matter

A few specific things to check before publishing any AI-generated ad:

Hands. AI still struggles with hands in both photo and video. If the generated image shows hands and they look wrong (extra fingers, weird positioning, blurred knuckles), regenerate. This is the single most common AI tell and it's immediately noticeable.

Text in images. If your ad includes text overlays, add them in post-production rather than generating them as part of the AI image. AI-generated text frequently has subtle spelling errors or inconsistent fonts that look wrong.

Product accuracy. If your product has specific branding (logo placement, color, packaging details), verify the AI got them right. AI sometimes approximates product details rather than reproducing them accurately.

Transitions in video. AI video models sometimes produce jarring cuts or unnatural transitions between scenes. For multi-scene ads, generate each scene separately and combine them in an editor rather than asking the AI to handle the transitions.

The bottom line

Making AI ads that don't look fake comes down to five decisions: use photo ads as your primary format, keep videos under 15 seconds, prioritize the hook over the avatar, curate ruthlessly rather than publishing everything, and match the creative style to the platform context.

The technology is good enough right now that the main variable isn't the AI's capability. It's the human's judgment in selecting, curating, and deploying the output. A brand with good taste running an average AI tool will produce better ads than a brand with no taste running the best AI tool.

Frequently asked questions

Why do AI-generated ads sometimes look fake?
Usually because of avoidable choices: video that's too long, backgrounds that are too perfect, or over-generating without curating. The technology itself is capable of much better results than most published output shows.

Should I use AI for photo ads or video ads first?
Start with photo ads. AI-generated static images are much closer to professional photography quality than AI video is, especially past the 15-second mark.

What's the most common AI tell to check for before publishing an ad?
Hands. AI still frequently gets fingers and hand positioning wrong in both photos and video, and it's the first thing viewers notice.
